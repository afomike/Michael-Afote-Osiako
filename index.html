Featured Project:
Fall Detection for Elderly Patients

Accidental Fall Prediction & Detection

Healthcare AI · Ensemble ML · Published IJCA 2025

The Problem

Falls are the second leading cause of accidental death globally, with adults over 65 at highest risk. In low-resource clinical settings, fall risk is assessed manually and infrequently — meaning high-risk patients go unmonitored between assessments. A real-time, automated early-warning system could meaningfully reduce mortality and caretaker response time.

Dataset & Features

The model was trained on a multi-sensor dataset containing physiological signals (heart rate, blood pressure, balance score) and environmental context (time of day, mobility history, floor surface). Features were engineered to capture both static risk factors and dynamic instability indicators, then standardised using a scikit-learn pipeline.

Model Selection & Reasoning

Six classifiers were benchmarked: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, SVM, AdaBoost, and Bagging SVC. Bagging SVC was selected for production because it delivered the best balance of precision and recall — critical in a medical context where a false negative (missed fall) carries far higher cost than a false positive. The ensemble's variance reduction over a single SVC meaningfully improved stability on unseen patient profiles.

Challenges

Class imbalance was the primary technical challenge: genuine fall events are rare relative to normal activity. SMOTE oversampling was applied to the minority class during training. A second challenge was inference latency — the model had to return a prediction and trigger an SMS alert fast enough to be actionable, which was addressed by pre-loading the serialised model at server startup rather than on each request.

Deployment

The trained model is serialised with joblib and loaded by a Flask REST API. The front-end sends sensor readings as a JSON payload; the API returns a risk classification and, if high risk is detected, dispatches an SMS to the registered caretaker via a third-party SMS gateway. The full stack is deployed on Render with a persistent worker process. The work was peer-reviewed and published in the International Journal of Computer Applications, Vol. 186, No. 78, 2025.

NLP

NaijaDetect — Nigerian Language Hate Speech Detector

Most content moderation models are trained exclusively on English — leaving over 200 million Yoruba, Hausa, and Igbo speakers with no automated protection online. NaijaDetect addresses this gap with a multilingual classifier that detects hate and offensive speech across all three languages in real time. A TF-IDF pipeline was tuned per language corpus; KNN was selected for its strong performance on limited labelled data typical of low-resource NLP tasks. Recommender

Flavr — Personalised Meal Recommendation Engine

Generic recipe apps ignore user preferences and return irrelevant results. Flavr builds a TF-IDF vector representation of each recipe's ingredients, tags, and cuisine, then ranks candidates using cosine similarity against the user's preference profile. RapidFuzz handles partial or misspelled ingredient queries. Authenticated users receive personalised recommendations with full nutritional breakdowns; session history is used to refine future rankings. The inference pipeline runs under 120ms per request in production.

Classification

PredictFlow — Road Traffic Congestion Classifier

Traffic management systems in African cities are largely reactive — signals change on fixed timers regardless of actual congestion. PredictFlow classifies road conditions into four severity levels from sensor-derived features (speed, vehicle count, time-of-day). The system exposes four interchangeable model backends — Naive Bayes, MLP, Decision Tree, and SVM — selectable per request, allowing planners to compare predictions and understand how different modelling assumptions affect outcomes. Biometrics

Cowry Face — Biometric Two-Factor Auth for Lagos BRT

Transit card fraud in Lagos costs operators millions annually — Cowry cards can be tapped by anyone who finds or steals one. Cowry Face introduces a facial verification step into the tap-flow: the user's face is matched against their enrolled embedding using dlib's 128-dimensional face descriptor, with OpenCV handling real-time frame capture. Only a matching card-plus-face pair grants access, making stolen cards functionally useless. The system runs on-device to avoid network latency at turnstiles.

Generative AI

Wofash GAN — Synthetic Nigerian Fashion Generation

Fashion datasets skew heavily Western — African fashion designers have little access to synthetic data for e-commerce or design tools. Wofash GAN is a DCGAN built from scratch in PyTorch and trained on the AFRIFASHION40000 benchmark (40,000 labelled images of African women's fashion) for 500 epochs on a Colab GPU. The generator and discriminator architectures use batch normalisation and LeakyReLU activations per the DCGAN paper; training stability was improved through label smoothing and learning rate scheduling. Generated samples show progressive mode coverage across distinct garment types and colour palettes.
